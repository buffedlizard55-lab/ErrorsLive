Methods & data audit

Reproducibility is the product. python3 tools/run_all.py rebuilds every published artifact from source files that live in this repo — the 24 archived feeds under data/raw/ and the nine replay CSVs under data/source/nydn/. No step depends on a scratch directory, a personal machine, or a network call. This page is the audit trail, including the things we got wrong.

1 · Every game re-verified against the league's own linescore

Each archived feed's final score is re-checked against https://statsapi.mlb.com/api/v1/game/{gamePk}/linescore — MLB's official record — for all 24 games, along with both teams' error totals and the official date. The result is committed as data/verified_linescores.json, which carries a per-game api link so any row can be checked by hand.

All 24 final scores match. All 24 team-error totals match the stratum each game was filed under in data/TARGETS.json (A = ≥3 team errors, B = exactly 2, C = ≤1), which independently confirms the stratification. All 24 official dates match docs/data/games.csv.

2 · The 222-game pool, independently counted

The pool was rebuilt from statsapi.mlb.com/api/v1/schedule?sportId=1&date=MM/DD/2026&gameType=R over the same 15 dates: 15 + 15 + 14 + 15 + 15 + 15 + 16 + 15 + 15 + 15 + 17 + 15 + 15 + 15 + 13 = 222, matching data/pool_summary.json.

Flagged irregularities found while re-counting the pool:
  • Three games appear on a slate date but carry a different officialDate: game 824424 (slate 2026-06-14 → officialDate 2026-09-04), 824664 (slate 2026-06-21 → 2026-08-06) and 823523 (slate 2026-07-18 → 2026-07-19, an ordinary make-up doubleheader). None are in the 24-game sample and the 222 total is unaffected.
  • Game 824581's linescore carries the official note “One out when winning run scored” (a walk-off). Its final 1–2 and eT = 2 are unaffected.
  • Not re-verified in this pass: the per-game error column of the 222-game pool (team_errors_total = 247 and the 0/1/2/3/4/7 distribution in pool_summary.json). Re-deriving it would need 222 individual linescore calls. It is retained as previously published and is listed as unverified in the roadmap.

3 · Sampling design (error-enriched — raking-weighted, NOT self-weighting)

The pool is 222 MLB regular-season games on 15 audited dates spanning June–September 2026, extracted from the official schedule+linescore endpoints only. The design is error-ENRICHED by intent (boring games are cheap to score; error games are the specimen) and is not self-weighting. Any pool-representative rate must apply the raking weights above; applying them reconstructs the pool's mean 1.113 team-errors/game as 1.258, the residual being sampling noise at n = 24. The fitted objects model P(class | batted-ball features) within a play, which is far less distorted than the game-level error rate — but the 1.91% base rate quoted on this site is the sample rate, not the pool rate, and is labelled that way wherever it appears.

4 · Transport-seam integrity log (4 healed games)

The original collection was chunked, and four payloads were damaged at a transport seam. The standing rule is never guess or invent a character: a seam that could be proven deterministically was healed; one that could not was resolved by dropping the straddling play and logging the loss. Every heal has a permanent log in data/raw/{pk}.integrity.txt.

5 · Dataset build

bip.csv — 1,271 rows

One row per batted ball in play that carried a Statcast hitData object. Columns, exactly as shipped: game_pk, play_index, event_type, macro_class, rbi, away_score, home_score, launch_speed, launch_angle, distance, trajectory, hardness, numeric_ok, review_overturned, review_type. Across 24 games and 1,868 plays. 12 rows have an incomplete vector and are quarantined via numeric_ok = 0 rather than zero-filled.

Label honesty

The 24 games contain exactly 24 plays labelled field_error, and all 24 carry a complete Statcast vector — there is no error-without-vector case in this archive, and none is claimed. The modelling set is 1,258 rows (1,271 minus 12 quarantined minus 1 catcher_interf play, which resolves to none of the four macro classes) → a 1.91% sample error rate. Errors charged within other eventTypes (a throwing error on a play scored a single) are not labelled error, so the observed rate is a lower bound.

games.csv — 24 rows

Official date, both teams' abbreviations, each team's runs/hits/errors, play and batted-ball counts, the integrity-heal flag, and a direct official_url for manual re-checking.

data_quality.json

Machine-readable audit: totals, the full event-type census, per-game play/battled-ball counts, the stratum tally, the missing-numeric quarantine (12), multi-hitData anomalies (0) and 'other'-class rows (1), plus the per-game score-check result against the official record.

6 · Modeling

StandardScaler + L2-regularized logistic regression (5 features + 8 indicators, 1,258 rows) for P{hit, error, fielder's choice, out}, and a parallel binary logistic for P(error). Logistic rather than a tree ensemble because the decision is a smooth threshold judgement (“ordinary effort”), the sample is small with 24 positives, and a calibrated probability surface is more honest than a black box at this n. Validation: 5-fold stratified out-of-fold (seed 20260929), AUC and per-class AUC, top-1 accuracy and per-class recall, a calibration table, an empirical trajectory × EV surface with Wilson 95% intervals, and 1,000× bootstrap CIs.

Reproduced bit-for-bit on Python 3.11.2 / numpy 2.4.6 / scipy 1.17.1 / scikit-learn 1.9.1 (pinned in requirements.txt).

7 · NYDN replay archive normalization

The nine published CSVs (2014–2018 regular season plus the 2014–2017 postseason files the archive actually contains) are vendored at data/source/nydn/ and parsed by tools/build_nydn.py, which repairs four real-world schema faults deterministically: mis-quoted game cells in the postseason files, comma-split player names, an “Record Keeping” status that is not in the published vocabulary (added, not dropped), four different malformed-date formats, and source typos such as the date 2014-04-31 (April 31st does not exist) and the timestamp 1;21 — both kept verbatim and flagged rather than silently “fixed”. Result: 6,361 rows → 3,067 overturned → 230 run-affected by the printed heuristic, with 2,688 source irregularities recorded in data/nydn_quality.json.

The first build of this page reported 6,168 rows. The 193 missing records were not a counting error in the archive — they were a parsing error here. 206 lines in the 2014 file begin with a stray double quote and 65 postseason lines open a quoted cell that is never closed; Python's CSV reader merged each of those records into its neighbour and dropped it, and a further 57 rows were discarded because their video cell was blank. Both classes are now repaired losslessly and counted. 6,362 source data lines minus one junk header fragment equals the 6,361 rows published. Separately, the season column was being filled with the source filename rather than the year, which silently broke the Season filter.

Re-verification. All 24 games were re-checked against statsapi.mlb.com/api/v1/game/{pk}/linescore on 2026-09-29 (24/24 pass, full ledger in data/verified_linescores.json, published on this page), and games 822801, 823441 and 822722 were spot re-checked a second time against the live endpoint while this section was written — all three still match the ledger exactly. tools/verify_official.py repeats the full check and refuses to report a pass when the network is unavailable (it exits 2 with “VERIFICATION NOT PERFORMED” rather than silently succeeding).
