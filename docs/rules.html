What the official sources say

Error — MLB's official glossary

“A fielder is given an error if, in the judgment of the official scorer, he fails to convert an out on a play that an average fielder should have made.”
“batters do not receive RBIs for any runs that would not have scored without the help of an error.”

MLB glossary: Error ↗

RBI — MLB's official glossary summary

“A batter is credited with an RBI in most cases where the result of his plate appearance is a run being scored. There are a few exceptions, however. A player does not receive an RBI when the run scores as a result of an error or ground into double play.”

The glossary is a summary. Rule 9.04 supplies additional detail and exceptions, so do not read that sentence as “every error means no RBI.”

Important error-play exception in Rule 9.04

Do not turn the glossary summary into an automatic ruling. Rule 9.04(a)(3) is an exception reference relevant to error-play RBI questions, but this page does not reproduce its operative conditions. The official 2026 PDF at page 115 is controlling. The full page was not independently extracted in this review, so verify the exact text and the play's counterfactual directly with the linked rulebook and official scorer before applying the exception.

How to read the site's candidate RBI notes

On the Live Board, a note appears for a scored run with a runner on second or third. These are conditional prompts based on available feed context; the feed does not establish the counterfactual required to settle an RBI.

Error candidate

No automatic RBI is assumed for an error-dependent run. The note points to Rule 9.04(a)(3) without trying to encode its full eligibility test; consult the official text and scorer. The model cannot determine a counterfactual RBI.

Hit candidate

A hit that causes a run can receive an RBI, subject to Rule 9.04. The displayed note is not a guarantee about the official stat line.

Fielder's-choice candidate

A run-scoring fielder's choice may receive an RBI, subject to Rule 9.04 and the scorer's decision. The candidate note does not convert a hypothetical ruling into a definite RBI.

Official scoring changes are distinct from umpire replay reviews

MLB's Official Scoring Changes page says changes may be made by the Official Scorer, Elias Sports Bureau, or following player/club review. Its current 2026 examples include an error changed to a single with an RBI change, and a fielder's-choice/error reclassification with no RBI. Those examples show why a category alone is not enough; they are not labels used to train the current model.

Official scorer changes alter statistical rulings. Umpire replay reviews can change safe/out or fair/foul calls and the scoreboard. The project keeps these two event types in separate datasets and pages.

Scope note. The current model is trained on the final macro-class recorded in the captured official feed. It does not predict the probability that an initial decision will later be changed, and it does not determine RBIs, runs, earned runs, or the scorer's judgment.
