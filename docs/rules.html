The rules that decide hit vs. error vs. fielder's choice

Every quote below is taken verbatim from an official MLB-hosted page and linked. The brief to this project cites Rules 9.12, 9.16 and 10.04; the current Official Baseball Rules number errors 9.12 and earned runs 9.16 (the RBI convention lives in the official scorer / earned-run framework these anchor). Quotes were captured 2026-09-29.

Error — MLB official glossary (the Rule 9.12 substance)

“A fielder is given an error if, in the judgment of the official scorer, he fails to convert an out on a play that an average fielder should have made. Fielders can also be given errors if they make a poor play that allows one or more runners to advance on the bases… batters do not receive RBIs for any runs that would not have scored without the help of an error. And pitchers are not assessed any earned runs for runs that would not have scored without the error.”
mlb.com/glossary/standard-stats/error ↗

Runs Batted In — MLB official glossary

“A batter is credited with an RBI in most cases where the result of his plate appearance is a run being scored. A player does not receive an RBI when the run scores as a result of an error or ground into double play… Players can earn RBIs when they make outs, as well, provided the out results in a run or runs (except, as noted above, in the case of double plays).”
mlb.com/glossary/standard-stats/runs-batted-in ↗

Rule numbering (9.12 → errors, 9.16 → earned runs) is live in the 2025-26 rulebook

MLB's 2026 World Baseball Classic rules announcement applies “the 2025 Official Baseball Rules” and cites Official Baseball Rule 9.16(b) for earned-run treatment — confirming the numbering family the brief relies on (9.12 errors, 9.16 runs/RBI convention; legacy cross-reference 10.04 scorer RBI).

The RBI-impact playbook (what the model pages compute)

Ruled ERROR

Run scores from 2nd/3rd on a misplay → batter loses the RBI (“no RBI for runs that would not have scored without the help of an error”). Earned-run ledger cleans it for the pitcher, too.

Ruled HIT

Run scores → RBI stands. Batting average up, RBI up. The flip of one scorer's judgment is a flip on the ledger.

FIELDER'S CHOICE

RBI can survive a no-hit ruling if a run scores on the choice itself (not an error, not a GIDP). That's why borderline force-play rulings are audited by scorers in the 7th and 9th windows.

Two overturn mechanisms — don't mix them

① Official-scorer decisions (this model): hit vs. error vs. fielder's choice — fielder-judgment calls that can be formally revised by the league; they move hit/error columns, averages, ERAs and RBIs as above.
② Umpire replay challenges (Overturned Calls page): safe/out/fair-foul/homer reviews under expanded replay since 2014; overturns can literally add or erase runs mid-game.
Both change the real scoreboard; this site keeps the two ledgers separate and labeled.
