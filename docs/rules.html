What the official sources say

Error — MLB's official glossary

“A fielder is given an error if, in the judgment of the official scorer, he fails to convert an out on a play that an average fielder should have made.”
“batters do not receive RBIs for any runs that would not have scored without the help of an error.”

MLB glossary: Error ↗

RBI — MLB's official glossary summary

“A batter is credited with an RBI in most cases where the result of his plate appearance is a run being scored. There are a few exceptions, however. A player does not receive an RBI when the run scores as a result of an error or ground into double play.”

The glossary is a summary. Rule 9.04 supplies additional detail and exceptions, so do not read that sentence as “every error means no RBI.”

The rule that decides the brief's RBI question — Rule 9.04, pages 115–116

Verbatim, 2026 Official Baseball Rules, Rule 9.04 (Runs Batted In), pages 115–116 (the rule opens on 115; subdivisions (b) and (c) are carried on 116):
“A run batted in is a statistic credited to a batter whose action at bat causes one or more runs to score, as set forth in this Rule 9.04.
(a) The Official Scorer shall credit the batter with a run batted in for every run that scores (1) unaided by an error and as part of a play begun by the batter's safe hit (including the batter's home run), sacrifice bunt, sacrifice fly, infield out or fielder's choice, unless Rule 9.04(b) applies; (2) by reason of the batter becoming a runner with the bases full (because of a base on balls, an award of first base for being touched by a pitched ball or for interference, obstruction, or shift restriction violation); or (3) when, before two are out, an error is made on a play on which a runner from third base ordinarily would score.
(b) The Official Scorer shall not credit a run batted in (1) when the batter grounds into a force double play or a reverse-force double play; or (2) when a fielder is charged with an error because the fielder muffs a throw at first base that would have completed a force double play.
(c) The Official Scorer's judgment must determine whether a run batted in shall be credited for a run that scores when a fielder holds the ball or throws to a wrong base. Ordinarily, if the runner keeps going, the Official Scorer should credit a run batted in; if the runner stops and takes off again when the runner notices the misplay, the Official Scorer should credit the run as scored on a fielder's choice.”

Read Rule 9.04 in the official PDF (opens at page 115) ↗

So: does an error mean no RBI?

Not always — and that is the point of Rule 9.04(a)(3). If the scorer charges an error but the run would have scored anyway from third base with fewer than two outs, the batter is credited with an RBI. The common “error ⇒ no RBI” summary in the glossary describes 9.04(a)(1) (“unaided by an error”); it is not the whole rule. A run that scores because of the error, with two outs, or on a force double play, is not an RBI ((b)(1) and (b)(2)).

Hit or fielder's choice?

Rule 9.05(b)(1), page 118: “The Official Scorer shall not credit a base hit when a: (1) runner is forced out by a batted ball, or would have been forced out except for a fielding error.” That sentence is the hit-versus-fielder's-choice boundary the model's four classes sit on. Rule 9.05(a) Comment (pages 117–118) adds: “In applying Rule 9.05(a), the Official Scorer shall always give the batter the benefit of the doubt.” Open the PDF at page 118 ↗

The scorer's real clock — Rule 9.01(a), pages 107–108

This is the authoritative answer to “can we see a pending scoring decision?” The rule names the phases a decision passes through. A public feed can show the first and last of them; it never shows the queue.

“Upon conclusion of an event in the field requiring a scorer's judgment, the Official Scorer will first make a ‘preliminary’ judgment call, generally using best efforts to do so in a timely fashion in line with the general pace of play, and no later than the start of the next plate appearance. Within 24 hours after a game concludes or is suspended, the Official Scorer will, at his or her discretion, render such preliminary judgments as ‘final,’ or revise the initial judgment call to become a final judgment. A Major League player or Club may request that the Commissioner's designee review a final judgment call of an Official Scorer made in a game in which such player or Club participated, by notifying the Office of the Commissioner in writing or by approved electronic means within 72 hours of a judgment becoming final. The Commissioner's designee shall have access to all relevant and available video and, after considering any evidence he wishes to consider, may order a change in a final judgment call if he determines that the final judgment of the Official Scorer was clearly erroneous. No judgment decision shall be changed thereafter.”

The quoted clock is the text of Rule 9.01(a) on printed page 108 (the rule opens on page 106 and the authority passage below ends at printed 107). Rule 9.01 also states the scorer's authority in the same words the model's honesty block uses: “The Official Scorer shall have sole authority to make all decisions concerning application of Rule 9 that involve judgment, such as whether a batter's advance to first base is the result of a hit or an error.” Read Rule 9.01 (page 106) ↗

What an error is — Rule 9.12, page 127

“An error is a statistic charged against a fielder whose action has assisted the team on offense, as set forth in this Rule 9.12. (a) The Official Scorer shall charge an error against any fielder: (1) whose misplay (fumble, muff or wild throw) prolongs the time at bat of a batter, prolongs the presence on the bases of a runner or permits a runner to advance one or more bases, unless, in the judgment of the Official Scorer, such fielder deliberately permits a foul fly to fall safe with a runner on third base before two are out in order that the runner on third shall not score after the catch;”

The same rule's Comment (printed pages 128–129; the quotations below span both) bounds the judgment the model is trying to predict: “Slow handling of the ball that does not involve mechanical misplay shall not be construed as an error… The Official Scorer shall not score mental mistakes or misjudgments as errors unless a specific rule prescribes otherwise. A fielder's mental mistake that leads to a physical misplay… shall not be considered a mental mistake for purposes of this rule and the Official Scorer shall charge a fielder committing such a mistake with an error… The Official Scorer shall not charge an error to a fielder who incorrectly throws to the wrong base on a play.” Read Rule 9.12 (page 127) ↗

Why that matters for a model. “Ordinary effort”, “in the judgment of the Official Scorer” and “mechanical misplay” are judgment standards. Contact physics (exit velocity, launch angle, distance, trajectory, hardness) cannot see where the fielders stood, how hard the ball was hit relative to the play, or whether the fielder's first step was clean. That is exactly why the model publishes a ranking with a stated precision — and why the site never prints a probability as though it were a ruling.

How to read the site's candidate RBI notes

On the Live Board, a note appears for a scored run with a runner on second or third. The note now applies the two conditions of Rule 9.04(a)(3) that the feed can see — fewer than two outs, and a runner on third base — and says plainly when the exception cannot apply. What it never claims is the third condition: that the runner from third “ordinarily would score”. That is the scorer's judgment, the feed does not record it, and no model here can supply it. RBI stakes, measured tabulates what the league actually credited in the collected window.

Error candidate

With a runner on third and fewer than two outs, the note says Rule 9.04(a)(3) can apply and quotes its counterfactual; otherwise it says the exception cannot apply and cites 9.04(a)(1)'s “unaided by an error”. Either way it is not a ruling.

Hit candidate

A hit that causes a run is credited an RBI under Rule 9.04(a)(1), subject to the 9.04(b) exceptions. The displayed note is not a guarantee about the official stat line.

Fielder's-choice candidate

A fielder's choice is one of the plays 9.04(a)(1) lists, so a run-scoring one can receive an RBI, subject to 9.04(b) and the scorer's decision. The candidate note does not convert a hypothetical ruling into a definite RBI.

Official scoring changes are distinct from umpire replay reviews

MLB's Official Scoring Changes page says changes may be made by the Official Scorer, Elias Sports Bureau, or following player/club review. Its current 2026 examples include an error changed to a single with an RBI change, and a fielder's-choice/error reclassification with no RBI. Those examples show why a category alone is not enough; they are not labels used to train the current model.

Official scorer changes alter statistical rulings. Umpire replay reviews can change safe/out or fair/foul calls and the scoreboard. The project keeps these two event types in separate datasets and pages.

Scope note. The current model is trained on the final macro-class recorded in the captured official feed. It does not predict the probability that an initial decision will later be changed, and it does not determine RBIs, runs, earned runs, or the scorer's judgment.
