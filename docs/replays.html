Overturned calls — and the runs they took off the board

A manager's challenge can erase a run that is already on the scoreboard. This page is built from two independent, machine-read sources and keeps them labelled:

1 · Modern ledger — official MLB feed

Every reviewed play in every ingested game, straight from statsapi.mlb.com (reviewDetails): review type, challenge, the final call, the score before and after, and two independent run-removal flags. Each row carries the play id the league itself publishes, which is what makes a per-play video link possible.

2 · Archive — 2014–18 replay rulings

The NY Daily News mlb-overturned-calls archive (6,361 rows) with its own run-removal field. Its video links are 12-year-old short links, so instead of pretending they work, the pipeline resolves every one of them and records what comes back — see the link-status panel and the full archive table.

Run-removal, stated as evidence rather than a claim

HARD flag. The feed's own runner movements say N runners reached the plate, but the scoreboard moved by fewer than N runs. That is only possible if a run was removed — no interpretation involved.

HEURISTIC flag. An overturned review whose own text or type is about a run: a home-run review overturned into a non-home-run, a runner put out at home, or a scoring/timing review on a play where a runner reached the plate. The exact reason is stored on the row and printed in the table below, so you can check it against the video. Both flags are published with their real counts — including when they are zero.
