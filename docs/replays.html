Overturned calls — and the runs they took off the board

A challenge can erase a run that is already on the scoreboard. This page is built from two independent, machine-read sources and keeps them labelled: the modern ledger straight from the league's own play-by-play, and the 2014–18 archive of replay rulings.

1 · Modern ledger — official MLB feed

Every reviewed play in every ingested game, straight from statsapi.mlb.com (reviewDetails): review type, challenge subject, the final call, the score before and after, and two independent run-removal flags. Each row carries the play id the league itself publishes — that is what makes a per-play video link possible.

2 · Archive — 2014–18 replay rulings

The NY Daily News mlb-overturned-calls archive with its own run-removal field. Its video links are 12-year-old short links, so instead of pretending they work, the pipeline resolves every one and records what comes back — see the archive page. Note: the repository named in the brief, github.com/buffedlizard55-lab/MLB-overturned-calls, returns HTTP 404 (checked 2026-09-29).

Run-removal, stated as evidence rather than a claim

HARD flag. The feed's own runner movements say N runners reached the plate but the scoreboard moved by fewer than N runs. That is only possible if a run came off — no interpretation involved. It is published even when it is zero: on the current window it never fires, which says the league's feed keeps the corrected movement and the corrected score consistent.

HEURISTIC flag. An overturned review whose own text or type is about a run: a home-run review overturned into a non-home-run, a runner put out at home, or a scoring/timing review on a play where a runner reached the plate. The exact reason is stored on the row and printed below, so every flag can be checked against the video. Both flags carry their real counts — including when they are zero.

How to re-check any row by hand

  1. Open the row's official feed link — that is statsapi.mlb.com/api/v1.1/game/<gamePk>/feed/live, the league's own JSON. Search the page for the play id; reviewDetails on that play holds isOverturned and reviewType.
  2. Open the watch link. If the league has a clip for that play id it plays there; if not, the page says "No Video Found" — that is an honest dead end, and the row is labelled.
  3. For the RBI consequence, read the rule quotations on Rules & RBI; they are copied verbatim from MLB's own glossary, with the source links.
