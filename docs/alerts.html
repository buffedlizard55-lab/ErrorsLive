Live alerts — what changed in the official feed, and when

This page polls the league's own play-by-play, scores every batted ball with the committed model, and diffs each poll against the previous one. When a captured ruling appears, changes, gains or loses an RBI, or is marked overturned, an alert is raised here, in your browser's notification area if you allow it, and in a ledger you can export. Nothing is uploaded: the polling and the diff both run in this tab.

What an alert is — and is not. MLB publishes no open scorer-decision queue, so no page can honestly claim to see one. An alert records an observable feed-state change between two captures of statsapi.mlb.com: a batted ball had Statcast contact data while result.eventType was absent, and a later capture had a different value. It is not proof that a decision was pending, who made it, or why. Rule 9.01(a) of the 2026 Official Baseball Rules describes the scorer's real clock: a preliminary judgment during play (generally no later than the start of the next plate appearance), then final or revised within 24 hours after the game, then a Club may appeal to the Commissioner's designee within 72 hours — and after that no judgment decision changes. Every alert below is a capture-time observation, not the scorer's clock.

Observation ledger recorded by CI (works without browser access to MLB)

GitHub Actions runs tools/watch_rulings.py every 15 minutes against live games (and re-reads the recent final-game window daily), then rebuilds data/alerts.json from what it actually observed. This table is that ledger — it is the fallback when this browser cannot reach the league's API, and the auditable record of what the scheduled watcher saw. It is not a substitute for watching a live game here.

How to check any alert by hand

  1. Open the row's official feed link: statsapi.mlb.com/api/v1.1/game/<gamePk>/feed/live, the league's own JSON. Find the play and read result.eventType, result.rbi and reviewDetails.
  2. Open the watch link (Baseball Savant's per-play page, the league's own video host) to see the play.
  3. For any run scored with a runner on second or third, read Rules & RBI: Rule 9.04 credits an RBI for a hit, infield out or fielder's choice, and — before two are out — when an error is made on a play on which a runner from third base ordinarily would score. That is the exception to "an error means no RBI", and it is why the model's conditional notes say check the scorer rather than predicting the stat change.
  4. Confirm the final ruling against MLB's Official Scoring Changes log.
