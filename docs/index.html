How will the scorer rule it?

A batted ball leaves the bat with a measurable launch speed (EV), launch angle (LA), distance, trajectory and contact hardness. Between contact and the official ruling, the scorer judges “ordinary effort” (MLB Rule 9.12). This page runs our calibrated model on those five Statcast inputs and returns a Scoring Likelihood Score /100 plus full outcome probabilities — the same quantities a live pipeline can read straight off the MLB Stats API feed.

Model: regularized multinomial + binary logistic on 1,258 Statcast batted balls from a self-weighting 24-game sample of a 222-game June–2026 pool (details & validation: Model). Live probabilities are pre-call judgments; the official scorer's ruling remains the source of record.

Why a hit-vs-error ruling matters — the RBI stakes

A runner on 2nd or 3rd whose run scores because of a misplay produces no RBI if the play is ruled an error — but the batter keeps the RBI if it's a hit, and a run-scoring fielder's choice can still earn one. MLB's official glossary is explicit: batters do not receive RBIs for runs that would not have scored without the error; no RBI on error or GIDP plays. Swing a borderline grounder one ruling direction or the other and you change a batting line, a fantasy scoreboard, arbitration numbers. Full rule walkthrough →
